# Gwaii Passage tidal turbine array

The Gwaii Passage array is a fictional demonstration tidal power installation
used here as a corpus-only fact: nothing about it exists outside this
encyclopedia, so a model that answers questions about it correctly must have
retrieved it.

## Configuration

The array comprises 14 horizontal-axis turbines of 1.8 MW rated capacity each,
sited in two rows across a channel 620 metres wide. Rated flow is 3.1 metres
per second. Nameplate capacity is 25.2 MW; measured capacity factor over the
2025 operating year was 38 percent.

## Notable design choices

Each turbine uses a two-bladed rotor with a fixed pitch, chosen because pitch
mechanisms dominated maintenance cost in earlier arrays. Yaw is passive: the
nacelle rotates on a slew bearing driven by the flow itself at slack water.

Cabling runs to a single subsea hub, designated GP-HUB-2, which feeds one
33 kV export cable to shore. The hub is the array's single point of failure and
is scheduled for a redundant twin in 2027.

## Operating history

The array entered service in March 2024. A blade-root crack found on turbine 9
in October 2025 was traced to vortex shedding from an upstream pier, and the
fix was to relocate that turbine 40 metres north rather than redesign the
blade.
