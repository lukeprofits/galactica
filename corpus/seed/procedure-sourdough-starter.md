# Establishing a sourdough starter

A starter is a stable culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. The goal
of the first week is not activity but stability.

## Day 1

Mix 50 g whole rye flour with 50 g water at 30 °C in a jar. Cover loosely and
hold at 24 to 26 °C. Rye is used at the start because its higher enzyme and
mineral content ferments faster than white flour.

## Days 2 to 3

Expect vigorous bubbling and a sharp, unpleasant smell around day 2. This is
usually Leuconostoc bacteria, not yeast, and it fades. Do not discard the
culture over the smell.

Feed once every 24 hours: discard all but 50 g, then add 50 g flour and 50 g
water.

## Days 4 to 7

Activity typically collapses on day 3 or 4 as the pH drops and the early
bacteria die off. This lull is the normal and expected midpoint, not a failure.
Continue feeding on schedule. Yeast activity returns as a predictable rise and
fall within 4 to 8 hours of feeding.

## Readiness test

The starter is ready when it reliably doubles within 4 to 6 hours at 25 °C and
smells of yogurt and green apple. A spoonful that floats in water is a weaker
signal than a timed doubling, because trapped gas persists after peak activity.

## Maintenance

Held at room temperature, feed daily. Refrigerated, feed weekly and give two
room-temperature feedings before baking.
