# Torque wrench

A torque wrench is a hand tool that applies a controlled, measured amount of
rotational force to a fastener. It exists because a fastener that is too loose
works free under vibration, while one that is too tight yields, strips its
threads, or crushes the joint it clamps.

## Types

Click-type (micrometer) wrenches store energy in a spring-loaded cam that
releases with an audible click at the set value. Beam-type wrenches deflect a
calibrated beam against a pointer and cannot be over-torqued by inattention,
because the reading is continuous. Digital wrenches read a strain gauge and
signal by tone and vibration; they are the only common type that can log a
tightening sequence.

## Accuracy and calibration

Consumer click-type wrenches are typically accurate to plus or minus four
percent of the set value in the middle 80 percent of their range, and are much
less accurate near the extremes of the scale. Calibration drifts with use and
with storage under spring load.

Calibrate a click-type wrench every 5,000 cycles or every twelve months,
whichever comes first. A wrench that has been dropped is out of calibration
until proven otherwise.

## Handling

Store a click-type wrench wound down to the lowest setting on its scale, never
at zero and never at maximum: zero can unseat the spring, and maximum leaves
the spring compressed and creeping. Do not use a torque wrench to loosen
fasteners, and do not extend the handle, which multiplies the applied torque
without changing what the mechanism measures.
