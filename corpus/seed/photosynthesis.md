# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and cyanobacteria convert
light energy into chemical energy stored in carbohydrate.

## Overall reaction

Six molecules of carbon dioxide and six of water, driven by light, yield one
molecule of glucose and six of oxygen:

    6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2

## Light-dependent reactions

In the thylakoid membrane, photosystem II splits water, releasing oxygen and
feeding electrons into an electron transport chain. The resulting proton
gradient drives ATP synthase. Photosystem I reduces NADP+ to NADPH. The net
products are ATP and NADPH, and the net waste product is molecular oxygen.

## Calvin cycle

In the stroma, the enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto ribulose
1,5-bisphosphate. The ATP and NADPH from the light reactions reduce the product
to glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate, three turns of the cycle producing one
three-carbon sugar. RuBisCO is the rate-limiting step and also the reason
photorespiration wastes carbon at high temperature and low CO2.

## C4 and CAM variants

C4 plants pre-concentrate carbon dioxide in mesophyll cells before handing it to
RuBisCO in bundle-sheath cells, which suppresses photorespiration. CAM plants
open their stomata at night, fixing carbon into malate for daytime use, which
trades growth rate for water conservation.
